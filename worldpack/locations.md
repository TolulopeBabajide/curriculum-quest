# Places — Oke-Ola

> **SYNTHETIC DATA.** Original places, representative of a real Nigerian community. Each place hosts a
> real-life situation that teaches a NERDC JSS1 Basic Science topic. The science is grounded in the
> public curriculum knowledge base; scenes are localized to the learner's own environment.

**Tags:** places, scenes, real-life
**Storyteller note:** each place presents a *practical situation*, not a magic gate. The learner
understands the science, then decides what to do — and the story responds.

---

## Home & compound  (Theme 1: Family Health · Living and Non-Living Things)
The learner's family compound at dawn. Chores to do, food to prepare, the yard to sweep.
- **Situation — A healthy morning:** someone in the house keeps falling sick. Through sanitation and
  nutrition (a balanced diet from local foods), the learner helps keep the family well.
- **Situation — What is alive?** sorting the things in the compound — the goat, the cassava plant, the
  bucket, the stone — into living and non-living, and naming states of matter (water, firewood, air).
- **Localize:** use the learner's actual home — flat or compound, what they eat, what's around them.

## The market  (Theme 1: Environmental Pollution)
The busy Oke-Ola market. Refuse is piling up; a gutter is blocked; flies and a bad smell.
- **Situation — The blocked gutter:** people are falling sick near the market. The learner works out
  what pollution is, what causes it here, its effects, and one practical way to reduce it.
- **Localize:** the learner's nearest market, gutter, or refuse spot.

## The stream  (Theme 1: Water pollution · Theme 2: Forces)
The stream at the edge of town, where families fetch water.
- **Situation — Is this water safe?** someone washed a sprayer upstream; the water looks cloudy.
  The learner reasons about water pollution and how to keep the water clean.
- **Situation — Carrying water home:** a practical look at force — pushing, pulling, lifting,
  and why a full bucket is harder to carry (a gentle lead-in to gravity).

## The family farm  (Theme 1: Living/Non-Living · Theme 2: Energy from the sun)
The family's small farm just outside the compound.
- **Situation — Why do the crops grow?** the sun's energy, living things, and what plants need.
- **Localize:** the learner's farm, garden, or even a potted plant.

## The health post  (Theme 1: Family Health — nutrition, drug abuse)
Nurse Bisi's small health post.
- **Situation — The balanced plate:** building a healthy meal from local staples; understanding
  drug and substance abuse and its effects, age-appropriately.

## The kitchen & the compound at night  (Theme 2: Energy · Renewable vs Non-Renewable · Theme 3)
Evening: cooking, lighting the lamp, and later, the night sky.
- **Situation — What powers our home?** firewood, charcoal, kerosene, gas, generator, solar, the sun —
  sorting renewable from non-renewable, and their use and misuse.
- **Situation — Under the stars:** looking up — the Earth in space, day and night, and why things fall
  down (gravitation and weightlessness).
- **Localize:** exactly what the learner's family uses for cooking and light.

## Starting scene (demo opener)
Morning at the learner's **home & compound**, with **Teacher Adaeze** stopping by on her way to school
and **Tunde** calling from the gate. After a short, friendly onboarding about the learner's real
surroundings, the first situation begins at home.
