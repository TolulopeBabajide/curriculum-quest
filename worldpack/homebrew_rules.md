# Game Rules — Curriculum Quest (Oke-Ola)

> **SYNTHETIC DATA.** Original lightweight mechanics for this project. The game is a real-life
> community simulation, so "challenges" are real situations, not fantasy battles.

**Tags:** rules, mechanics, knowledge-check, real-life

## Core mechanic: the Real-Life Challenge (understanding earns progress)
Progress comes from understanding science well enough to act on a real situation:

1. **Set the scene** — the Storyteller describes a real situation in the community, localized to the
   learner's own environment (their home, market, stream, farm, kitchen).
2. **Teach** — Teacher Adaeze explains the relevant topic using cited curriculum content + a real,
   local example.
3. **Challenge** — a neighbour (trader/farmer/nurse) poses ONE practical question grounded in that
   cited content ("The gutter by your market is blocked and people are sick — what is happening, and
   what is one thing your community can do?").
4. **Answer** — the learner responds in their own words, about their own surroundings.
5. **Verify** — the neighbour compares the answer to the cited source and rules:
   - **Correct** → the situation improves in the story; the learner earns a **Skill** and **Confidence**.
   - **Partial** → a gentle hint; try once more (no penalty).
   - **Wrong** → lose **1 Confidence**; Teacher Adaeze re-teaches briefly; try again.
6. **Resolve** — the Storyteller narrates the real-world outcome and what changes in Oke-Ola.

## Confidence (the progress stat)
- The learner starts with **3 Confidence**.
- A fully wrong answer (after a hint) costs 1 Confidence.
- Reaching 0 never ends the game — Teacher Adaeze re-teaches, the learner rests, and resumes.
  Learning is never "game over."

## Skills (badges, named for real competence)
Earned per topic mastered, named practically:
- "Clean & Healthy Home" (Family Health)
- "Pollution Stopper" (Environmental Pollution)
- "Living World Observer" (Living and Non-Living Things)
- "Energy Smart" (Energy / Renewable vs Non-Renewable)
- "Force Mover" (Forces)
- "Sky Watcher" (The Earth in space / Gravitation)

## Real-life action (practical extension — optional, powerful)
After a correct answer, the Storyteller may invite a **real action**: "Before our next chat, sweep one
corner of your compound / ask your family what your light costs / look at the moon tonight." This turns
the lesson into something the learner does in real life — the heart of practical learning.

## Localization rule (always on)
Every scene, example, and challenge uses the learner's stored environment (home type, cooking/lighting
source, nearby market/farm/stream). If a detail is unknown, the Storyteller asks once, then remembers.

## Dice (flavour only — optional)
A `roll_dice` tool exists only for small narrative colour (the weather, who's at the market today) —
never to decide whether learning succeeds. Understanding decides that.

## Safety & tone
- Age-appropriate (~10–12), warm, never harsh. Handle topics like drug abuse simply and responsibly.
- Never reveal a challenge's expected answer before the learner attempts it.
- Tell the learner at the start that Teacher Adaeze, the neighbours, and Tunde are AI characters.
