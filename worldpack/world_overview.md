# Community Overview — Oke-Ola

> **SYNTHETIC DATA.** Oke-Ola is a fictional Nigerian community invented for Curriculum Quest. It is
> a *representative* stand-in, not a real town — and the game **localizes** every scene to the real
> learner's own environment (see learner-context personalization). Educational content is grounded in
> the public NERDC curriculum, cited.

**Tags:** community, setting, overview, real-life, nigeria
**Player-known:** yes

## The setting
Oke-Ola is an everyday Nigerian community — the kind of place a JSS1 student actually lives in. It has
homes with compounds, a busy market, family farms, a stream that runs past the edge of town, a small
health post, and a school. People cook with firewood, kerosene, gas, or sometimes a generator or solar
panel. Some keep chickens and goats; many grow yam, maize, vegetables, or cassava.

This is not a fantasy world. It is **your world**, told as a story — so that the science you learn is
the science you can see, touch, and use the same day.

## The big idea: science is practical
In Oke-Ola, science isn't a subject in a book — it's how you keep your home clean, why the gutter by
the market makes people sick, what your family burns to cook, and what you see when you look up at the
night sky. The learner moves through real situations and uses Basic Science to understand and improve
them. Understanding has real consequences in the story: a cleaner stream, a healthier family, a
brighter compound.

## Personalization (this matters)
At the start, the learner is asked about their *own* surroundings — village/town/city, what their
family uses for cooking and light, whether there's a market, farm, or stream nearby. From then on,
every example, story, and challenge is told using **their** real environment. A child in a city flat
and a child in a farming village will learn the same curriculum through different, familiar examples.

## The places of Oke-Ola (and the science they hold)
1. **Home & compound** — family health (sanitation, nutrition); living and non-living things.
2. **The market** — environmental pollution (refuse, blocked gutters) and how it affects health.
3. **The stream** — water pollution; forces (fetching and carrying water).
4. **The family farm** — living and non-living things; energy from the sun; food and nutrition.
5. **The health post** — family health (balanced diet, drug and substance abuse).
6. **The kitchen & the compound at night** — energy sources, renewable vs non-renewable energy;
   the night sky for the Earth in space and gravitation.

## The people (synthetic)
- **Teacher Adaeze** — the school's Basic Science teacher; patient, encouraging. The learner's mentor.
- **A helpful neighbour** — flexes to the scene (market trader Mama Nkechi, farmer Baba Sule, Nurse
  Bisi at the health post). Poses a real, practical problem and judges the learner's reasoning fairly.
- **Tunde** — the learner's friend and classmate; curious, funny, learns alongside the player.

## Tone
Warm, story-rich, encouraging, and recognisably Nigerian. Mistakes are part of learning — they earn a
gentle hint and another try, never embarrassment. Suitable for ~10–12 year-olds.
